Largest daily movements

Largest absolute day-to-day percentage-point change per selected series within the analysis window. Both dates must be present in the window.

Interpretation of storage movements

A change in stored volume is not water consumption or pump throughput. Rainfall, inflows, releases, evaporation and operational changes can affect storage. This dataset alone cannot establish their individual contributions. No flood, safety or cybersecurity alert is inferred.

MEASUREMENT DEFINITIONS

Units and calculation rules

Storage percentage
The publisher’s reported percentage. Values above 100% remain valid source observations.
Stored volume
The publisher’s reported megalitres (ML). One ML equals one million litres.
Percentage-point change
Later storage percentage minus earlier storage percentage. This is distinct from a relative percentage change.
Maximum drawdown
The largest decline from an observed peak to a subsequent observed trough within the selected window.
Monthly movement
Last observed percentage minus first observed percentage in the month. Partial months are flagged.
Rolling mean
Mean of available observations in the preceding 7 or 30 calendar days, including the current date. Counts identify incomplete windows.
SOURCE INTERPRETATION

Coverage and operating context

The original export contains Baroon Pocket, Hinze, Little Nerang, North Pine (Lake Samsonvale), Somerset, Wivenhoe and SEQ Water Grid CSVs. The grid is an overlapping regional aggregate and is not added to individual dam volumes.

Seqwater’s source notes describe reduced full supply levels for North Pine, Somerset and Wivenhoe. Storage percentages therefore require site-specific operating context when comparing dams.

Daily records may omit intraday peaks. The source contains no observation time, rainfall, releases, demand or pump-energy measurements. Storage changes describe net movement; this dataset alone does not establish its cause.

One annual cycle supports a description of monthly variation, not confirmation of recurring seasonality. Correlations are exploratory associations between matched daily changes.
